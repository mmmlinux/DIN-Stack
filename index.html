The screen

  • Library (left): the object types you can place. « hides it for more drawing space.
  • Drawing (middle): your DIN rails, parts, wires and cables, at their real size in millimetres.
  • Inspector (right): the settings of whatever is selected. With nothing selected, it shows the diagram's title block (revision, date, author, notes).
  • Lists (bottom): wires, cables, bus bars, parts, wire profiles and cable profiles. Drag the top edge to resize them, or use the arrow at the right to hide them.
  • Top bar: the File, Edit and Help menus, undo and redo, and the diagram's title (click it to rename). Settings… is in the Edit menu.
  • Bottom left of the drawing: the diagram's pages, one tab each, and + to add one.
  • Bottom right of the drawing: the Select and Wire tools, buttons for how wires and cables are drawn, the selection filter, and zoom.
  • Right-click anything, or empty space, for what you can do with it.

Saving and files

  • Your work is saved automatically in this browser, and is there next time you open DIN Stack in it.
  • File → Save (Ctrl+S) downloads the diagram as a file, to keep a copy or open it on another computer. Save as… (Ctrl+Shift+S) lets you choose the file's name and folder (in browsers that can; otherwise it asks for a name). File → Open… (Ctrl+O) opens one.
  • File → New diagram starts an empty one, keeping your libraries. File → Open the example loads a small 24 V control panel to explore. Both can be undone.
  • The start screen appears when DIN Stack opens: continue, start a new diagram, open a file or load the example. If nothing is clicked within 10 seconds, it carries on with your diagram. Turn it off with its tick box or in Settings, and open it any time from Help → Start screen.
  • Example links such as #mechatronics open a ready-made example in its own workspace, saved separately from your usual diagram.

Moving around

  • Drag empty space to pan (or hold Space and drag). Use the mouse wheel to zoom, or the zoom buttons at the bottom right. F fits the diagram to the window.
  • With the Measure tool (M), drag across the drawing to measure a distance in mm and inches. The ends snap to terminals and to the corners and edges of parts and rails. Hold Shift to keep the line level or upright, or Alt to turn snapping off. A measurement at an angle also shows its distance across and down. Click or press Esc to clear it.

Selecting

  • Click to select. With the Select tool (V), clicking a terminal selects the wires, bus bars and cable on it. Shift-click adds to the selection, and Shift-drag on empty space draws a selection box. Ctrl+A selects everything; Esc clears the selection.
  • With several things selected, the inspector has a section for each kind (wires, bus bars, cables, parts, rails, notes) with the settings they share. A change there applies to all of that kind. A setting they don't share shows as Mixed.
  • The selection filter (the funnel at the bottom right) limits what you can pick, e.g. only wires, or wires and cables. With it on, clicks pick only those kinds, and dragging on empty space draws a box that picks them up, including wires, bus bars and cables with an end in the box. Space or Esc turns it off.
  • Show only these wires (right-click selected wires, or in the inspector) hides every other wire, to follow a few at a time. Click Show all at the bottom right to bring the rest back. Printouts and exported images always include every wire.

Adding parts

  • Click an object in the library, or drag it onto the drawing. With a part selected, the new one lands flush beside it in the build direction.
  • Search the library with the box at its top. Categories start folded: click a name to open it, or use the button beside the search box to open or fold them all.

Moving parts

  • Drag parts to move them. They snap flush against neighbours and centre on DIN rails. Hold Alt to place freely.
  • Hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) while dragging to push the parts touching it on that side along too, e.g. to open a gap in a terminal strip. Anything past a gap stays put.
  • The arrow keys nudge the selection by one grid step (Shift for ten). R and Shift+R rotate it.

A part's settings

  • Tag (e.g. TB101), Colour, and Description, which is printed on the part.
  • Type swaps the part for another type. The new one takes the old one's place: it stays on its rail, keeps flush neighbours flush, and pushes neighbours aside if it's bigger. Wires stay connected where the new type has the same terminals; the rest are removed (you can undo).
  • Terminals lists each terminal with its wires (click one to select it) and a box for a connector label (see Circuit names).

Terminal strips

  • Select a terminal block and use Repeat under Strip in the inspector to add numbered copies beside it.
  • Build direction (Left → right or Right → left) sets which way strips grow: new parts added beside the selected one, repeats, and packing.
  • To select a strip, use All of this type or All “TB…” under Strip, or Shift-click and Shift-drag.

Several parts at once

  • Renumber tags numbers the selected parts in position order, with a prefix, start number, step and order of your choice. Their wires stay connected.
  • Pack side by side lines them up with no gaps. Align lines up their tops, middles, bottoms or lefts. Both are also on the right-click menu.

DIN rails

  • Right-click empty space → DIN rail here. Set its Length and Profile (35 or 15 mm) in the inspector.
  • Parts sitting on a rail move with it. Fit length to parts trims or stretches the rail to the parts on it.

Text notes

  • Right-click empty space → Text note here. Set its text, size, colour and bold in the inspector. Notes are printed and exported with the drawing.
  • Group notes: select some parts or rails, right-click → Group note. The note gets a bracket across the items, with a line to the note, and follows them when they're all moved. Drag the note to another side of the items and the bracket moves there. The bracket starts just clear of the items' wire labels; drag its long line in or out to place it, or set its distance in the note's panel, or click Clear the labels there to fit it again after rewiring. To link an existing note, select it with the items → Link note to the selected items.
  • Turn a note a quarter turn with R (or Shift+R the other way), the turn buttons in its panel, or its right-click menu. Its Angle sets any angle, e.g. 45°. A note turns about its middle.

Copying

  • Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V copy and paste parts, rails and notes, with the wires, bus bars and cables between them. Ctrl+D duplicates the selection. Right-click empty space → Paste here pastes at that spot.

Pages

  • A diagram can have several pages, for example the back panel, the door and a junction box. The tabs at the bottom left of the drawing switch between them, and + adds one.
  • Double-click a tab to rename the page. Right-click it to rename, duplicate, move or delete the page.
  • To move parts, rails or notes to another page, select them, right-click and pick the page under Move to page, or New page. They keep their place, and the parts on a rail go with it.
  • Wiring between pages: start a wire on one page, open the other page and click the terminal it goes to. Cables and bus bars work the same way while you pick their terminals.
  • A wire or cable to another page is always drawn broken at each end, its label saying where it goes and on which page, e.g. W3 → S1-13 (Door). Untick Page names under the wires or cables button at the bottom right (or in Settings) to leave the page out. Right-click it and choose Go to other end to jump there.
  • Hold the mouse over a wire, its label or a cable to see what it is: its name, where each end goes and on which page, its circuit, colour, size, profile and notes.
  • Snapping, selection boxes, Ctrl+A and measuring work on the page you're on. The lists cover the whole diagram, with a Page column; clicking a row on another page opens that page.
  • Print / PDF prints each page on a sheet of its own. SVG and PNG export the page you're on.

Libraries

  • The drop-down at the top of the library picks which one is shown: This project, Built-in, each library you've loaded or imported, or All libraries.
  • This project holds the types made or changed in this diagram: new types, duplicates, and edited copies of types from other libraries.
  • + opens the library manager, which loads, unloads and refreshes the libraries that come with DIN Stack (e.g. Phoenix Contact, AutomationDirect), or imports a library file.
  • ⋯ exports the library shown as a file, with your wire profiles, to import into other diagrams.
  • File → New diagram and the start screen's New diagram keep your libraries. Untick Keep my libraries on the start screen to start with just the built-in one.

Drawing wires

  • With the Wire tool (W), drag from one terminal to another, or click one terminal and then another. Esc cancels a wire you've started.
  • With the Select tool (V), clicking a terminal selects what's on it (its wires, bus bars and any cable plugged in) without starting a wire, so you can see where they go. A terminal with nothing on it selects its part. Right-click a terminal → Start a wire here still works.
  • The tool buttons are at the bottom right of the drawing.
  • Terminals are referenced as tag plus terminal name, e.g. TB101-1. The separator is in Settings.
  • New wires are numbered W1, W2…, and take a colour and size from the circuit they join, or a guess from the terminal names (e.g. +24V, 0V, PE).

A wire's settings

  • Click a wire's label or line to set its wire number, profile, colour, stripe, size, conductor and notes.
  • Stripe adds a tracer stripe, e.g. red with a white stripe, listed as "Red/White".
  • Swap ends (right-click) swaps a wire's From and To.
  • Moving a wire's end: right-click the wire near the end to move, or its label at that terminal, and pick Move end at…, then click the terminal it goes to instead. This works for a cable's cores too.
  • Finding the other end: right-clicking a wire or cable offers only what can be done at the end you clicked, plus Go to other end, which scrolls the drawing to it. A cable with a free end shows the options for both ends instead.
  • Finding the other end: a selected wire's two ends are ringed in blue, and the rings stay the same size however far you zoom out. A wire drawn as stubs also gets a dashed line between its ends. Clicking a wire in the list below does the same.

How wires look

  • The wire button at the bottom right of the drawing picks Stubs (a short stub at each terminal), Straight lines or Curved lines.
  • Labels shows where each wire goes at its terminals, e.g. TB101-1. Wire names adds the wire's number: W3 → TB101-1, or at the middle of a line.
  • Named circuits show picks whether a named circuit's wires are labelled with its name (+24V) or with where they go.
  • One wire can be drawn differently from the rest: set its Draw as (or right-click → Draw wire) to Straight line, Curved line or Broken (a labelled stub at each end). Handy for breaking just the long runs, or drawing a short jumper as a line.
  • The printout and exported images look the same as the drawing.

Wire profiles

  • A wire profile is a named wire type, e.g. "+24 V DC: red, 16 AWG, stranded". Pick one under Profile in a wire's panel, for one wire or several selected.
  • Favourites: only the profiles starred ★ are offered in the Profile drop-down and the right-click menu's Wire profile, so they stay short. All wire profiles… there opens a window with all of them to search and pick from. Star or unstar a profile there, in Edit… or in the Wire profiles list at the bottom. New profiles start as favourites.
  • Editing a profile updates all its wires. Edit them with Edit… next to a wire's profile, or from the Wire profiles list at the bottom, where clicking a profile selects its wires.
  • Changing a wire's colour or size by hand makes it a custom wire again.

Matching the circuit

  • A new wire added to a circuit that already has wires matches them (profile, colour, size, conductor) and keeps following: change the feed wire and the matching wires change with it.
  • Changing a wire by hand stops it following; tick Match the circuit to turn it back on. Bus bars work the same way for their colour.

Wire standard

  • Wire standard in Settings switches between American (AWG, black hot, white neutral, green ground) and metric (mm², brown line, blue neutral, green/yellow earth). Existing wires and profiles are converted.

Circuits

  • A circuit is everything connected together: wires, bus bars, and terminals joined inside a part (both sides of a terminal block, say).
  • Selecting a wire lights up the rest of its circuit in lighter blue, on the drawing and in the wire list.
  • Which terminals are joined inside a part is set with Link in the object type designer.
  • A terminal's Description in the object type designer (e.g. +24 VDC) shows when the mouse is over that terminal on every part of the type.

Selecting a circuit

  • Select whole circuit (in a wire's panel or its right-click menu) selects everything wired to it.
  • When the circuit's name reaches further, through a connector label or other wiring with the same name, there are two buttons instead: Select all of “+24V” and Select connected wires, for only what's physically wired to this one.
  • Select daisy chain picks only the wires and bus bars joined at the same terminals, such as a jumper chain along a strip, without crossing to the other side of the blocks.
  • Each button says how many it selects.

Circuit names

  • Select a wire and type a Circuit name (e.g. +24V). The whole circuit takes the name, and it shows in the wire list, CSV and printout.
  • Circuit's labels show picks whether this circuit's wires are labelled with its name or with where they go. This wire's label shows overrides that for one wire. Right-click wires → Label shows does several at once.

Connector labels

  • Select a part and type a label next to a terminal under Terminals. It isn't drawn: it shows when the mouse is over the terminal. You don't need to draw a wire from where the supply comes from.
  • Terminals and wires with the same name count as one circuit.

Joining named circuits

  • If a wire, bus bar, cable or connector label would join two differently named circuits (say +24V to 0V), you're asked first.
  • Pick Call it all +24V (or the other name) to join them under one name, Keep both names to join them as +24V / 0V, or Don't join to leave things as they were.

Bus bars

  • Right-click empty space → Bus bar… (or right-click a terminal → Bus bar from here…). Click the terminals to bridge, or drag along a row, then press Enter or Done.
  • Terminal blocks have two bus bar slots (the small squares in the middle) that take bus bars but not wires.
  • To bridge alternate blocks, sweep along a row of slots, then click Keep 1st, 3rd… or Keep 2nd, 4th….
  • A dot marks each block a bar connects to, so a bar passing over a block without a dot skips it.
  • Edit terminals… in a bus bar's panel picks its terminals again. Give it a label, colour and notes there too.

Cable basics

  • A cable is a sheath with cores inside. Each end fans out into cores on terminals, plugs into a cable connector, or is free, running off the drawing.
  • Bundling wires: select the wires that run in one cable and right-click → Bundle into a cable. The sheath fans out into the coloured cores at each end.
  • A cable with one end on the drawing: right-click empty space → Cable… (or right-click a terminal → Cable from here…), click the terminals its cores go to, and press Enter. Fill in Goes to (e.g. "Pump P1") to label the free end. Its cores are referenced as C1-1, C1-2…
  • Drawing the other end later: right-click the cable → Fan out to terminals…, then click the terminal each core goes to, in order. The bar at the top says which core is next.
  • The Ends section of a cable's panel changes either end: plug in, fan out, unplug, leave free or add cores.
  • Moving a cable's end: right-click the cable near the end to move and pick Move end… (also in the Ends section of its panel). Click the terminal each core moves to, in core order, or drag along a row. Keep leaves a core where it is, and so does clicking Done before picking one for it. The other end stays where it is. To move a plugged-in end, use Plug into another connector….
  • Using only some cores: when a cable whose profile lists its cores fans out from a plugged-in or free end, each terminal you click gets the Next core shown in the bar at the top. Pick another core there, or click Skip to leave one spare. The panel on the right lists each picked terminal with its core, to change or swap them, and the drawing shows each terminal's core number. Afterwards, the Cores section of the cable's panel can still change which core each wire is.
  • Cores connected at one end only: when landing a cable's other end, click Skip to leave a core not connected at that end. In the cable's panel, each wire's drop-down says which ends it's connected at (Both ends, End A only or End B only); a wire at one end only is marked not connected through and drawn at that end only. Pick Both ends, or Connect through… in the wire's panel, then click the terminal at its other end. Taking the last wire off one end of a cable leaves that end free.

How cables look

  • Drag the orange dots on a selected cable to move where its cores fan out. They move with the parts at that end.
  • A cable drawn Broken has two dots at each end: one where the cores fan out and one where the tail stops, so each tail can be put wherever it fits. Reset fan-out points puts them all back.
  • The cable button at the bottom right draws cables Full length or Broken: a short tail at each end labelled with where the other end goes. A cable's own Draw setting overrides that. Cable names adds its name, e.g. C1 → K1.
  • A shielded cable is drawn with a dashed outline along its sheath.
  • Finding the other end: a selected cable's ends are ringed in blue: the connectors it plugs into, or the terminals its cores land on. The rings stay the same size however far you zoom out, and a broken cable also gets a dashed line between its ends.
  • Unbundle (keep wires) removes a cable but keeps its wires.

Cable connectors

  • A cable connector is a socket that takes a cable's plug rather than single wires, such as an RJ45 port on an Ethernet switch, a motor socket, or a D-sub on a fan-out block.
  • Drag from one connector to another to plug in a cable between them, like a patch lead.
  • Drag from a connector to a terminal for a cable plugged in at one end with its cores landing on terminals at the other, such as a motor cable from a drive's U, V, W and PE.
  • Right-click a connector for Plug in a cable…, Select cable or Unplug the cable.
  • Several connectors at one end: an end of a cable can plug into more than one connector, alongside its loose cores, e.g. a motor cable with a power plug, an encoder plug and two cores on terminal blocks at the drive end, and one connector at the motor. Use Add a connector… for that end (in the cable's Ends section, or right-click the cable → End A or End B). The end branches at its fan-out point, a length of sheath to each connector, and each connector can be unplugged on its own.
  • When making a new cable with Cable…, the first connector you click is the far end; any more connectors go at the near end, with the cores.
  • For a cable from outside the enclosure, put a loose part such as the Mains plug at the far end.

Cable profiles

  • A cable profile is a named cable type: sheath colour, type (e.g. Cat6 S/FTP), Shielded, Twisted pairs, notes, and the cores it contains.
  • Pick one under Cable profile in a cable's panel. Editing a profile updates all its cables.
  • Like wire profiles, only the favourites ★ are offered in the drop-down and right-click menu; All cable profiles… opens the rest.
  • They're listed under Cable profiles at the bottom: click one to select its cables, or Edit… to change them.

Cores

  • Click Cores on a cable profile to list its cores in order, each with a marking (e.g. 1, 2…), colour, stripe, size and conductor. A cable's cores take those looks, core 1 first.
  • A core changed by hand keeps its own look until the profile is picked again. With no cores listed, the wires in a cable keep their own looks.

The cable library

  • Ready-made cable types with their cores: Ethernet, PROFINET, PROFIBUS, 9-core serial, M8 and M12 sensor cables, control and D-sub 25 cables, power, motor and instrument cables.
  • Open it from From the cable library… in a cable's Cable profile drop-down, the cable button at the bottom right, or the Cable profiles list. Add puts a copy in this drawing; Update replaces a copy that differs.
  • New drawings start with no cable profiles. Editing a drawing's copy doesn't change the library.

Designing a type

  • + New part opens the designer. Set the name, library, category, tag prefix and size in millimetres.
  • Click on the preview to add a terminal there, or add a whole row or grid at once.
  • Add a grid places blocks like a 2 × 16 header or an IC. Numbering can wrap by rows or columns, go around like an IC, or use row letters (A1… B1…), starting from any corner.
  • Drag over empty space to select several terminals, then drag one to move them all. Shift+click adds to the selection, the arrow keys nudge, Ctrl+D copies, Delete removes and Ctrl+Z undoes.

Editing a type

  • Right-click a part → Edit object type…, or use the edit button in its panel. Editing a type updates every part that uses it.
  • Editing a type from another library saves an edited copy in This project, and the parts switch to it. The original isn't changed.
  • The library guide covers every setting, a worked example, sharing libraries and the file format.

Terminal settings

  • Link: give terminals the same value when they're joined inside the part, such as both sides of a terminal block or a power supply's two +V terminals. Wires on them count as one circuit.
  • Type: Wire for an ordinary terminal, Bus bar slot for a point that takes bus bars only, or Cable connector for a socket that takes a cable's plug.

Mounting

  • DIN rail parts snap onto rails. Set Mounting to Loose for parts that aren't on a rail, like connectors on the enclosure wall.
  • The built-in Enclosure connectors group has some ready-made loose ones (tag prefix X).

The lists

  • The tabs at the bottom list the diagram's wires, cables, bus bars, parts, wire profiles and cable profiles. Click a row to select it on the drawing.
  • The Filter box shows only the rows containing what you type.
  • The parts list ends with a count of each type.

Printing and exporting

  • File → Print / PDF… (Ctrl+P) opens the print settings first: paper size, orientation (or Automatic) and margins, how big the drawing comes out, and what to print. Then Print… opens the print window; pick Save as PDF there for a PDF, and the same paper size as in the settings.
  • Size of the drawing: Fit each page on one sheet; Real size, shrunk to fit one sheet if too big; Fit to the paper's width; or a Set scale, where 100% is real size (1:1). With the last two, a page too big for one sheet goes on over several, each labelled with its row and column.
  • Each sheet of drawing gets a page to itself, with a heading at the top that's the same on every sheet (title, page, revision, and which sheet it is when a page takes several), and the drawing centred under it. The notes go under the heading on the first sheet. The lists start on a new page. Choose which drawing pages to print (all, the one open, or none) and which lists, and whether to print the heading.
  • Paper sizes and margins follow the wire standard: American uses Letter, Legal, Tabloid… and inches, metric uses A4, A3… and mm. Switching the standard in Settings switches the paper to the nearest size.
  • File → SVG image is true to scale, for printing at 1:1 or using in other drawings. PNG image is a picture of the drawing. Both are of the page you're on.
  • File → Wire list (CSV), or CSV above the wire list, downloads the wire list for a spreadsheet.

Settings

Edit → Settings…. Saved with the diagram, except where noted.

  • Drawing: snap grid, how wires and cables are drawn, labels, text heights, snapping to neighbours and rails, and terminal names.
  • Wiring: the wire standard (American or metric) and how terminals are referenced (TB101-1, TB101:1…).
  • Building strips: the build direction.
  • Starting up: whether the start screen appears when DIN Stack opens. This one is saved in the browser.

Right-click menus

  • Right-click a part, terminal, wire, bus bar, cable, rail or note for the things you can do with it.
  • Right-click empty space to add a rail, note, bus bar or cable there, paste, undo or fit the view.
  • Right-clicking something in a multiple selection keeps the selection, so the menu applies to all of it.
  • Entries with › open a submenu: hover over them, click them, or press →. ← goes back.

Getting started
  1. Place parts

    Click a type in the library on the left, or drag it onto the drawing. Parts snap flush against each other and centre on DIN rails. Right-click empty space on the drawing to add rails, notes, bus bars and cables.

  2. Wire them up

    Drag from one terminal to another. Each terminal is labelled with where its wire goes, and the wire list below fills in as you go.

  3. Edit anything

    Click a part, wire or bus bar to change it in the inspector on the right. Right-click it for more.

  4. Keep your work

    Everything is saved in this browser automatically. Use File → Save for a file you can open elsewhere. The File menu also prints and makes images.

Included libraries

Ready-made libraries that come with DIN Stack. Load adds one to this diagram as its own library, picked with the drop-down at the top of the library. Refresh loads it again to pick up changes; it's highlighted when a newer version is out. Unload removes it, along with any parts that use its types. All of these can be undone.

Wire and cable profiles

A profile is a named wire type. Wires assigned to a profile take its colour, stripe, size and conductor, and editing a profile here updates all of them. Changing a wire by hand makes it a custom wire again. Only the favourites (★) are offered in the drop-downs and right-click menus; the others are under All wire profiles… there.

Cable profiles

A cable profile is a named cable type. Add the ones this drawing uses from the cable library, or make your own. Each has its sheath colour, type (e.g. Cat6 S/FTP), whether it's shielded or has twisted pairs, any notes, and the cores it contains. Cables assigned to a profile follow it, and editing it here updates all of them. Click Cores to list its cores in order, each with a marking, colour, stripe, size and conductor: a cable's cores then take those looks, core 1 first. With no cores listed, the wires inside a cable keep their own looks. As with wire profiles, only the favourites (★) are offered in the drop-downs and right-click menus.
